What is being done to fix this?

Updated Legislation

To assist counties that feel like they are underserved in their DMA, several Representatives from Congress added provisions to the STELA Reauthorization Act of 2014 to allow counties to petition the FCC to review a county’s inclusion or exclusion in a particular DMA (note 11). One of the qualifying factors includes “whether modifying the local market of the television station would promote consumers' access to television broadcast station signals that originate in their state of residence”. This allows counties to petition the FCC to have modifications made to the DMA and permit channels from outside of the DMA, but can serve that community, to be broadcast via cable and satellite systems. One county in Southwest Colorado, La Plata County, did just that in October of 2016 (note 12). They claim that Nielsen has orphaned their county by assigning them to the Albuquerque DMA and have “deprived them of the ability to receive...its preferred in-state television broadcast stations”.

As recently as this year, Franklin, Elbert, Hart, and Stephens Counties, Georgia, have successfully petitioned and received relief in their petition to be aligned in the Atlanta DMA vs their currently assigned Greenville/Spartanburg, SC - Asheville/Anderson, NC DMA (note 13).

Both the Colorado and Georgia petitions faced appeals from TV stations within their respective assigned DMAs to keep these counties but in the case of the Georgia counties, they were successful in their petition. In the case of La Plata County, one TV station is allowed to be broadcast in the city of Durango, CO. KUSA, provides their feed as specified hours - aligned with their local news broadcast - to the town and they broadcast it over the local television station’s subchannel. The city of Durango gives away television antennas to permit their residents to watch this local feed of the Denver NBC station. They are still working through their petition for the other Denver area (note 12).
