What are "Orphan" Counties?

Orphan Counties Defined

As officially defined by the FCC, Orphan Counties are counties that are “served exclusively, or almost exclusively, by television stations coming from a neighboring state.” (note 5) These are fringe areas that by physical location can make sense to be added to a DMA but may involve incorporating areas from other states or time zones that are different from the named DMA area. For example, in our DMA, the Boston-Manchester, NH market, the area includes Windham County in Southern Vermont. How many news stories can you think of that come from the town of Brattleboro, VT on the local Boston news stations?

As technology progressed through the 80’s and television viewing continued to increase cable systems started to develop to bring premium content to the homes of viewers who were willing to pay for additional television content. As such, cable systems were beginning to be adopted in the average American home. No longer were families relying on their television antenna for content. As a part of the “package” from the cable systems, they included feeds from the viewer’s local television channels. To protect broadcasters from the competition from the cable systems the FCC created a rule called the “must carry” rule to require cable systems (and later satellite systems) to carry all local television stations to ease the access to these stations for the consumer. This introduced local television stations to consumers to TV stations that they may not have been able to watch before due to the geography or strength of the signal for that particular station.

Examples of Orphan Counties

La Plata County, Colorado (note 6), note 7)

La Plata County is a part of the Albuquerque, NM DMA and thus does not receive coverage from Colorado.

Early County, GA

Early County, GA is not only separated from Georgia but it's also in the Eastern Time Zone whereas the Dothan DMA is in the Central Time Zone. This time difference must be confusing for viewers.

Johnson and Campbell Counties, WY

These Wyoming counties are a part of the Denver DMA but are significantly distant from the rest of the DMA. Either the Casper-Riverton DMA or Rapid City DMA would make more sense for these counties.

Esmeralda County, NV

Esmeralda County is a part of the Los Angeles DMA but is significantly far away from Los Angeles and is in another state. It's feasible to assume that this county does not get adequate local coverage about the issues happening in Nevada. Not to mention local weather coverage. It would make more sense for this county to be a part of the Las Vegas DMA given it's close proximity to that city/DMA.

Digital Gerrymandering?

In his Medium article, Jason Charney argues that these DMA configurations could be a form of gerrymandering via media markets (note 8). While this is a personal blog post, I can understand why he comes to this conclusion. In the CJR “Orphan Counties” (note 9) article, some level of this is demonstrated through the arguments made by the New Mexico Broadcasters Association and the local Albuquerque stations that fought La Plata County’s petition to leave the Albuquerque market. In doing so, it would reduce the population numbers for the DMA thus potentially allowing the market to fall from it’s ranking of #47 to a lower number. Local stations include their market’s ranking as a factor in the prices they charge entities to advertise on their airwaves. With the Albuquerque market being in one of the top 50 largest markets, they can charge a higher fee (note 10). If they drop in their rankings, they will have to reduce their price to one that more matches their potential new ranking. Similar to political gerrymandering, they want to keep people in the right places to give the station (political = candidate or party) the number of people for population rankings.
